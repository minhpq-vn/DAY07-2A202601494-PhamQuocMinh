# Total price and consent

Before a purchase, the consumer must be clearly informed of the total price, including taxes and additional charges. An online buyer must explicitly acknowledge that placing the order creates an obligation to pay.

Additional services such as express delivery or gift wrapping require the buyer's consent. A pre-ticked box is not valid consent, and money collected that way must be reimbursed.

# Card fees and authentication

EU traders generally cannot add a surcharge for consumer credit or debit cards. Limited exceptions include American Express, Diners Club, and business or corporate cards; even then the fee cannot exceed the trader's actual processing cost. Online payments above EUR 30 require at least two authentication elements drawn from possession, knowledge, and identity.

# Currency and price discrimination

When a trader offers payment in the buyer's home currency, it must disclose the conversion cost as a percentage markup over the latest European Central Bank exchange rate. Consumers may refuse and pay in local currency. EU nationals and residents cannot be charged more solely because of nationality or country of residence, although delivery costs and country-specific website prices may differ.
