# Withdrawal and legal guarantee

When a business sells goods or services online or through another distance channel, the consumer generally has fourteen days to return the item or cancel the service without giving a reason. The seller must also provide a minimum two-year legal guarantee for faulty, misdescribed, or improperly functioning goods.

# When the seller owes redress

A retailer is bound by public statements made in advertisements or labels. Redress may be claimed when an item does not match its description, lacks the advertised qualities, is not fit for its normal or agreed purpose, lacks normal quality and performance, or was installed incorrectly by the seller or because the instructions were deficient. A buyer cannot claim redress for a particular defect that the seller clearly disclosed before sale.

# Available remedies

The buyer may request repair, replacement, price reduction, or contract cancellation and full reimbursement without charges for postage, labour, or materials. In most countries repair or replacement comes first. Price reduction or refund follows when repair or replacement is impossible, disproportionately expensive, significantly inconvenient, or not completed within a reasonable time.
