# Legal guarantee

If goods are faulty or do not look or work as advertised, the seller must repair or replace them at no cost. When that is impossible, or cannot be done within a reasonable time and without significant inconvenience, the buyer is entitled to a full or partial refund.

The minimum legal guarantee lasts two years from receipt of the goods. If a defect appears within one year of delivery, it is presumed to have existed at delivery unless the seller proves otherwise. Some EU countries extend this reversed burden of proof to two years. Purchases from private individuals are not covered by these rules.

# Commercial and second-hand guarantees

A commercial warranty may offer more protection but cannot reduce the minimum legal guarantee. Second-hand goods bought from a professional seller are also covered for two years, although some countries allow buyer and seller to agree to a shorter period of at least one year.

# Digital content and services

Digital content and services have a minimum two-year legal guarantee when faulty, not as advertised, or not working as expected. The supplier must fix the problem within a reasonable time, without charge and without significant inconvenience; otherwise the buyer may seek a price reduction or end the contract.
