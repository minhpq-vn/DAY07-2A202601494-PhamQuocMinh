# Payment setup

A payment service provider should provide a secure environment and access to the payment methods selected by the seller. A hosted payment page leaves security and compliance updates to the provider; an integrated gateway gives the seller more control but requires the seller to manage those updates and compliance.

For cross-border sales, the provider should support payment methods in target countries and the currencies the business accepts. Payment service providers must be registered in their home member state and with the European Banking Authority; sellers can verify them in national registers.

# Customer payment rights

At the start of checkout, the seller must identify accepted payment methods. Before the order, it must disclose the total product or service cost and any payment fees. Extra payments require explicit customer consent; pre-ticked boxes are prohibited. The seller cannot charge more for a payment method than it costs to offer it.

After payment confirmation, the seller should email the customer to confirm completion of the transaction. Online selling also creates tax and VAT duties that depend on the customer, location, goods, and services involved.
